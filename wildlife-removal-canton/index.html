At your Canton property

A yard sighting is a starting clue

Canton’s Etowah River Park and Heritage Park connect to trails along the river. Seeing an animal outdoors does not, by itself, show that it has entered your home. Start with where the signs stop: at the fence, near a door, or inside a room.

City of Canton parks and trails

If your lot meets trees or a green space, look from the ground for damaged vents or trim. Note sounds and the time they happen. Do not climb onto the roof or follow an animal into cover. An inspection can test whether there is a route inside before you agree to repair work.

Start with the room, then work outward

Suppose you hear scratching above a bedroom after a day of yard activity. Write down which room, which wall, and when it starts. Share a photo of any damage you can see without a ladder. That gives the team a clear place to begin; the sound alone does not identify the animal.

Wildlife help

Animal signs to discuss in Canton

Raccoons

A raccoon crossing the yard is one clue. Repeated sounds above a ceiling or a torn roof edge need a closer look. Keep the two kinds of evidence separate.

About raccoons

Gray squirrels

Watch from the ground for the point where a squirrel leaves a branch and reaches the house. Note the route without cutting branches or blocking a hole first.

About gray squirrels

Bats

A bat flying above a trail is different from one entering a building. If you see an exit at dusk, record its location from a safe distance.

About bats

Rats

Tell us whether signs are near the garage, pantry, or lower wall. Leave droppings alone; a clear note about the room is more useful than a disturbed trail.

About rats

Mice

Small sounds can travel through a wall. List the rooms where you hear them, and mention stored food or pet food nearby so those areas can be checked.

About mice

Birds

A nest in a yard tree is not the same as nesting in a vent. Share the exact location before anyone removes material or closes the opening.

About birds

Pest control

What to note before you call

Ants

Map the trail from the counter toward a door or window. A photo of the ants helps the team choose the right control plan.

Roaches

Note whether you see them in one damp room or in several parts of the home. Tell us about leaks that still need repair.

Termites

Save photos of mud-like tubes or shed wings. Do not assume that all wood damage has the same cause.

Mosquitoes

After rain, note water held in pots, covers, and other yard items. Include places you cannot reach safely.

Spiders

Tell us where webs keep returning and whether you have seen the spider. Those details help narrow the inspection.

Fleas and ticks

Record where people or pets spend time outdoors. Tell the team about pet care already in use before any treatment is planned.

Pest and Wildlife Removal in Canton, Georgia FAQ

Common questions

Should I call about one animal crossing my Canton yard?

You can ask for help identifying what you saw. One sighting alone does not establish a home-entry problem. Repeated activity, damage, or signs indoors give the team more to check.

Does living near the Etowah River mean my attic has wildlife?

No. The parks and river explain the local setting, not the condition of a house. A home inspection must find its own evidence.

What should I record if I only hear a noise?

Note the room, time, and how long it lasts. Say whether it is scratching, a heavy step, or fluttering. Sound can be a clue, but it is not a firm animal ID.

Can I seal a hole before the visit?

Wait until the opening and any animals inside have been checked. Closing an active route too soon can trap animals. Ask how removal and repairs will be timed.
