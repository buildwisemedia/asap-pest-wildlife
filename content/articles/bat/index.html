Bat removal in Georgia: plan around maternity season

Bats leaving a roof opening at dusk can be a sign of a roost inside. Before you seal the gap, find out whether bats—and any young—are still using it. The right timing matters.

What Georgia says about timing

Georgia’s Department of Natural Resources says bat exclusions should be avoided from April 1 through July 31. Young bats may be unable to fly and could be trapped inside. If exclusion must take place during that period, the state says it must be done by a licensed Nuisance Wildlife Control Operator, a professional permitted to handle wildlife conflicts. Georgia DNR bat guidance

The state’s linked management guide limits maternity-season exclusion to cases involving a human health risk. A license alone does not make every job suitable. August 1 is not an automatic start date, either. Ask the operator to check for young, identify the species, and confirm suitable weather and current requirements before work begins. Georgia bat management guide (PDF)

If you notice signs during July, you can arrange an inspection and discuss a safe schedule. If you notice them at another time of year, have them checked then.

Signs to share with an inspector

  • Bats leaving the same gap around dusk.
  • Droppings beneath a possible roost or entry point.
  • Staining around a roofline opening.
  • Repeated sounds or an unusual odor near the attic.

These clues need to be checked. Do not handle a bat or crumble droppings to identify them.

Avoid contact with bats and droppings

Bat droppings can be linked to Histoplasma, the fungus that causes histoplasmosis. People can become ill after breathing in spores from contaminated material. This does not mean every pile of droppings contains the fungus. Avoid disturbing a buildup and ask about safe cleanup. CDC histoplasmosis guidance

If someone has had contact with a bat—or a bite or scratch cannot be ruled out—seek prompt advice from a health professional. They can assess possible rabies exposure. Do not wait for a pest inspection to get medical advice. CDC clinical rabies guidance

What to ask before work starts

Ask how the operator will find entry points, protect any young, choose a safe time, and check that bats have left before sealing openings. One-way devices can let bats leave while keeping them from returning when conditions allow their use. Georgia bat management guide (PDF)

Your estimate should state which repairs, cleanup, follow-up visits, and warranty terms apply. Avoid treating a fixed date or a broad promise as a substitute for a plan for your building.
